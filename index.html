Research and build your regional bloom plan

In the practice garden, the research was done for you. Now you’ll use credible regional sources to identify plants that fit your own climate, garden conditions, and pollinators, then build a 6–8 plant bloom plan.

Oregon learners: Start with Oregon State University Extension and other Oregon/regional resources. Outside Oregon: use the same process, beginning with your state or provincial Extension service, land-grant university, or ecoregional resources.

2. Learn the research process

For each candidate plant, try to answer six practical questions:

  1. Is this plant appropriate for my region?
  2. When does it usually bloom locally?
  3. What pollinators use it?
  4. What site conditions does it need?
  5. What tradeoffs or cautions should a gardener know?
  6. Can an ordinary gardener realistically obtain it?
Use more than one kind of source.

A regional Extension publication may be best for garden performance, a flora or government database for native range, a pollinator organization for pollinator relationships, and a nursery or seed company for current availability. Commercial sources are useful, but should not be your only evidence for ecological claims.

3. Research candidate plants

Research up to 10 candidates. You’ll choose 6–8 for your final plan. You do not need to fill every slot.

4. Build your regional bloom plan

Choose 6–8 of the candidates you researched. Aim for overlapping bloom from February 15 through September 30, while also considering site fit, maintenance, availability, and your local evidence.
